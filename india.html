NZ-India Free Trade Agreement

Taking your business to India

Test it properly. Structure it properly. Then go.

New Zealand and India signed a Free Trade Agreement on 27 April 2026. It has not come into force yet - both countries still need to complete ratification, and the tariff reductions phase in from there rather than landing all at once.

That gap is the opportunity, and it is shorter than it looks. Testing whether the market stacks up, incorporating in India, registering for tax in both countries and setting intercompany pricing takes months, not weeks. The businesses trading from day one will be the ones who used this window instead of waiting for the announcement.

Should we go?

Before you commit a dollar, we build the business case.

Market sizing and realistic revenue assumptions. Landed cost modelling including duty, freight and GST. Distributor versus own-entity comparison, with the numbers behind each. What it actually costs to establish and run a presence for three years. Where the money goes if it goes wrong.

2

How should we structure it?

If the answer is yes, structure determines your tax outcome for the next decade, and it is expensive to unwind.

Choosing your vehicle: a private limited subsidiary, branch office, liaison office, or LLP. The differences are substantial. A branch office needs regulatory approval, is restricted in what it can do, and is taxed as a foreign company at a materially higher rate than an Indian domestic company. For most New Zealand SMEs the subsidiary is the right answer, but you should know why, not just be told.

How you fund it - debt or equity - and what that means at both ends. Intercompany agreements written before trading starts rather than reconstructed at audit. Transfer pricing policies that will hold up to both Inland Revenue and India's tax authority. And modelling what a rupee of Indian profit actually lands as in New Zealand after Indian tax, withholding and imputation.

For a captive, there is now a simpler route

India reset its safe harbour rules this year: software development, IT-enabled services, KPO and contract R&D are a single category at a uniform 15.5% margin on operating expenses, with the eligibility threshold lifted from Rs 300 crore to Rs 2,000 crore. Acceptance is automated, and the election runs five years. For most New Zealand captives that replaces a benchmarking study with an election, at a fraction of the cost and none of the dispute risk.

Eligibility turns on characterisation rather than size. The Rs 2,000 crore ceiling is roughly NZ$400 million of transaction revenue, so it is not a constraint for a New Zealand business. What decides it is whether the Indian entity is a genuinely low-risk service provider - work directed from here, IP owned here, commercial risk carried here. Where decisions and IP have migrated to India over time, the entity is no longer routine and the regime does not fit. If you are not sure which side of that line you sit on, that is the first thing to establish, and it is not a question to answer from a form.

The trade-off is that electing safe harbour bars the Mutual Agreement Procedure under the tax treaty for that transaction - certainty in exchange for a relief route you would probably never use, but a decision to make knowingly. It is filed by 30 June of the first year, so it belongs in the conversation at incorporation, not after the first return.

3

Keep us right

The obligations that follow you home, which most New Zealand accountants do not look for.

Controlled foreign company (CFC) rules. Once you own an Indian subsidiary, Inland Revenue can tax you on its profits even before any of the money comes back to New Zealand. There is an exemption where the Indian entity is genuinely trading rather than sitting on passive income, but the test behind it has to be applied and documented every year - not once at the start.

Limits on interest deductions (thin capitalisation). There are caps on how much of the group's debt you can hold in New Zealand to fund the Indian operation, and on how much of that interest you are allowed to deduct here.

Transfer pricing documentation for the New Zealand parent, not just the Indian entity. The pricing between the two companies has to stand up at both ends, and the paperwork proving it sits here as well as there.

Foreign tax credits and getting profits home. Making sure tax already paid in India is credited against your New Zealand bill, and planning how and when profits are brought back.

Consolidated group reporting. Combining the Indian entity into your New Zealand accounts - translating rupees to dollars, and stripping out the transactions between the two companies so nothing is counted twice.

Plus ongoing coordination with your Indian compliance calendar, so nothing falls between the two jurisdictions.

Two things worth knowing now

The FTA changes tariffs, not your tax position

Market access and duty rates are one thing; permanent establishment risk, withholding tax and transfer pricing are governed by the NZ-India Double Tax Agreement and each country's domestic law. They need looking at separately, and the FTA does not help you with them.

India replaced its entire income tax legislation on 1 April 2026

The Income-tax Act 2025 took effect for the 2026-27 financial year. The underlying principles largely carry over, but every section reference has changed, which means a good deal of the guidance still circulating online now cites repealed provisions. Worth checking who is advising you, and from what.
